Marathon Prediction Trend

ⓘ
Forecast over time at a maximal effort, tracking your fitness. Y-axis is reverse-scaled — lower = faster. Model: Bayesian durability model — log-time regressed on distance (durability βd), fitness (chronic load) and effort (HR); the marathon is an extrapolation past your longest race, so a drift-informed wall penalty widens the band — as does how unsure we are of your assumed race-day effort (the effort-schedule panel below), wider the further past the races we've measured. Read it as: a fitness-sufficiency trajectory under a well-executed effort — not race-day odds (weather/pacing/fuelling are extra). Distinct from the Daniels VDOT/pace zones (how sharp your engine is now). The durability-collapse plot below shows the same model on distance.
What time would my current fitness deliver, over time? Lower = faster. The line is the durability model's median tracking your chronic load; the shaded band is its 90% credible interval. Dots are your efforts projected to the goal distance, coloured by their own distance (blue = short → red = long); the diamond is today's forecast with its 90% whisker.

The forecast, decomposed
log t = α + βd·log(dist/goal) + φ·fitness + κ·effort
α baseline — marathon-equiv time at avg fitness (CTL 50) & threshold effort (HR = LTHR); the three slopes adjust from here βd durability — time vs distance φ fitness — chronic load (CTL): avg daily training load over the prior ~4 weeks κ effort — avg HR vs LTHR (per 5 bpm; how hard the run was)
Each coefficient below is the slope of its term — its covariate vs marathon-equivalent time with the other two netted out, posterior median + 90% band (greyed when the data can't inform it). The white rise/run triangle reads the slope; points are coloured by distance (blue → red); a red ring marks your most-recent run; hover a point for its distance and marathon-equivalent.

VDOT Trend — Anchor vs Garmin

ⓘ
Two estimates of aerobic capacity. Anchor VDOT (solid line) is performance-anchored — derived from any running activity (training or race) that meets the criteria: 5–25 km, avg HR ≥ LTHR, consistent pacing. Larger dots = race-day efforts. Garmin VO2max (dashed) is a physiology estimate from wrist HR — less reliable, shown for comparison. When they disagree, trust the anchor VDOT. Model: Daniels' formula. Assumes: population-average running economy (±10–15% between runners), so VDOT is a performance index, not a measured VO₂max — your real economy is tracked in Aerobic Efficiency below. Also assumes a near-maximal, evenly-paced effort on a flat, fair-conditions course; hills/heat/trail lower the figure (handled as confidence, not dropped).
Where is my aerobic engine, in performance terms? Anchor VDOT (solid) comes from any race-effort run; Garmin (dashed) is a reference.
